# St. Augustine on the Salvation of all Men

  * [samshmn](<https://www.samshmnthelogy.net/profile/samshmn78303/profile>)
  * Dec 22, 2025
  * 11 min read


In his work called [**_The Enchiridion_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1302.htm>) , St. Augustine denied that 1 Timothy 2:4 teaches that God desires the salvation of every single human individual. Believing that God’s will is perfect and immutable, and that no one can ever thwart his will, St. Augustine reasoned that this proves that God didn’t desire to save every person. If God had wanted every man to be saved then God would have sovereignly brought this to pass, since no one could stop him from doing so. Rather. St. Augustine understood 1 Timothy 2:4 to mean that God desires the salvation of all kinds and classes of human beings, from all walks of life.

I cite the words of this blessed saint where he elaborates on this point. All emphasis will be mine. 

 _Chapter 100._**_The Will of God is Never Defeated_** _, Though Much is Done that is Contrary to His Will._

These are the great works of the Lord, sought out according to all His pleasure, and so wisely sought out, that when the intelligent creation, both angelic and [ _human_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , [ _sinned_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , doing not His [ _will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>) but their own, **He used the very will of the creature which was working in opposition to the Creator's will as an instrument for carrying out His**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**, the supremely Good thus turning to good account even what is**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**, to the condemnation**** _of those whom in His_**[** _justice_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08571c.htm>)** _He has_**[** _predestined_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12378a.htm>)** _to punishment_****, and to the**[** _salvation_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>)******_of those whom in His mercy He has_**[** _predestined_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12378a.htm>)** _to_**[** _grace_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>). For, as far as relates to their own consciousness, these creatures did what God wished not to be done: **but in view of God's omnipotence, they could in no wise effect their purpose. For in the very fact that they acted in opposition to His**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**,****_His_**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)** _concerning them was fulfilled_**. And hence it is that  _the works of the Lord are great, sought out according to all His pleasure,_ **because in a way unspeakably strange and wonderful, even what is done in opposition to His**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)******_does not defeat His_**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)** _. For it would not be done did He not permit it (and of course His permission is not unwilling, but willing); nor would a Good Being permit_**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)** _to be done only that in His omnipotence He can turn_**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)** _into good_**.

 _Chapter 101. The Will of God, Which is Always Good, is Sometimes Fulfilled Through the Evil Will of Man._

Sometimes, however, a man in the goodness of his will desires something that God does not desire, even though [ _God's_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) [ _will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>) is also good, nay, much more fully and more surely good (**for His**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**never can be**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)): for example, if a good son is anxious that his father should live, when it is God's good will that he should die. Again, it is possible for a [ _man_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) with [ _evil_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>) will to desire what God wills in His goodness: **for example, if a bad son wishes his father to die, when this is also the**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**of**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)**. It is plain that the former wishes what God does not wish, and that the latter wishes what God does wish** ; and yet the filial [ _love_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09397a.htm>) of the former is more in harmony with the good [ _will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>) of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , though its desire is different from God's, than the want of filial affection of the latter, though its desire is the same as God's. So necessary is it, in determining whether a man's desire is one to be approved or disapproved, to consider what it is proper for [ _man_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , and what it is proper for [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , to desire, and what is in each case the real motive of the will. **For God accomplishes some of His purposes, which of course are all good, through the**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**desires of**[** _wicked_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**men: for example, it was through the**[** _wicked_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**designs of the**[** _Jews_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08399a.htm>)**, working out the good purpose of the**[** _Father_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)**, that Christ was slain and this event was so**[** _truly_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>)**good, that when the Apostle Peter expressed his unwillingness that it should take place, he was designated**[** _Satan_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm>)**by Him who had come to be slain**. How good seemed the intentions of the [ _pious_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12748a.htm>) [ _believers_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05769a.htm>) who were unwilling that [ _Paul_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11567b.htm>) should go up to Jerusalem lest the [ _evils_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>) which [ _Agabus_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01200a.htm>) had foretold should there befall him! **And yet it was God's purpose that he should suffer these**[** _evils_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**for preaching the**[** _faith_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05752c.htm>)**of**[** _Christ_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08374c.htm>)**, and thereby become a**[** _witness_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15677a.htm>)**for Christ. And this purpose of His, which was**[** _good_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06636b.htm>)**, God did not fulfill through the good counsels of the**[** _Christians_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03712a.htm>)**, but through the**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**counsels of the**[** _Jews_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08399a.htm>)**;****_so that those who opposed His purpose were more_**[** _truly_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>)** _His servants than those who were the willing instruments of its accomplishment_**.

__

_Chapter 102._**_The Will of the Omnipotent God is Never Defeated, and is Never Evil_** _._

__

But however strong may be the purposes either of [ _angels_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01476d.htm>) or of [ _men_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , whether of good or bad, whether these purposes fall in with the [ _will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>) of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) or run counter to it, **_the_**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)** _of the Omnipotent is never defeated; and His_**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)** _never can be_**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>) ; because even when it inflicts [ _evil_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>) it is just, and what is just is certainly not [ _evil_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>). The [ _omnipotent_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11251c.htm>) [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , then, whether in mercy He pities whom He will, or in judgment hardens whom He will, **is never**[** _unjust_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08010c.htm>)**in what He does, never does anything except of His own**[** _free-will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>)**,****_and never wills anything that He does not perform_**.

__

_Chapter 103. Interpretation of the Expression in I Tim. II. 4 "Who Will Have All Men to Be Saved."_

Accordingly, when we hear and read in Scripture that He  _will have all_[ __men__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) _to be saved,_ although we [ _know_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08673a.htm>) well that all [ _men_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) are not saved, **we are not on that account to restrict the omnipotence of**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)**, but are rather to understand the**[** _Scripture_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/bible/index.html>) ,  _Who will have all_[ __men__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) _to be saved_ , ** _as meaning that no man is saved unless God wills his_**[** _salvation_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>)** _: not that there is no man whose_**[** _salvation_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>)** _He does not will, but that no man is saved apart from His_**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)** _; and that, therefore, we should_**[** _pray_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12345b.htm>)** _Him to will our_**[** _salvation_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>)** _, because if He will it, it must necessarily be accomplished_**. And it was of [ _prayer_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12345b.htm>) to God that the apostle was speaking when he used this expression. **And on the same principle we interpret the expression in the**[** _Gospel_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06655b.htm>) :  _The_[ __true__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) _light which lights every man that comes into the world_ : **not that there is no man who is not enlightened, but that no man is enlightened except by Him. Or, it is said** ,  _Who will have all_[ __men__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) _to be saved_ ; **not that there is no man whose**[** _salvation_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>)**He does not will (for how, then, explain the fact that He was unwilling to work**[** _miracles_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10338a.htm>)**in the presence of some who, He said, would have repented if He had worked them?), but that we are to understand by**** _all_**[** __men__**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)** _,_********_the_**[** _human race_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)** _in all its varieties of rank and circumstances — kings, subjects; noble, plebeian, high, low, learned, and unlearned; the sound in body, the feeble, the clever, the dull, the foolish, the rich, the_**[** _poor_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12327a.htm>)** _, and those of middling circumstances; males,_**[**_females_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15687b.htm>)** _, infants, boys, youths; young, middle-aged, and old men; of every tongue, of every fashion, of all arts, of all professions, with all the innumerable differences of will and_**[** _conscience_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04268a.htm>)** _, and whatever else there is that makes a distinction among_**[** _men_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)** _. For which of all these classes is there out of which_**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)** _does not will that men should be saved in all nations through His only-begotten Son, our Lord, and therefore does save them; for the Omnipotent cannot will in vain, whatsoever He may will?_** Now the apostle had enjoined that [ _prayers_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12345b.htm>) should be made for all [ _men_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , and had especially added,  _For kings, and for all that are in authority_ , who might be supposed, in the [ _pride_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12405a.htm>) and pomp of worldly station, to shrink from the humility of the [ _Christian_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03712a.htm>) [ _faith_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05752c.htm>). Then saying _, For this is_[ __good__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06636b.htm>) _and acceptable in the sight of_[ __God__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) _our Saviour_ , that is, that [ _prayers_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12345b.htm>) should be made for such as these, he immediately adds, as if to remove any ground of despair,  _Who will have all_[ __men__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) _to be saved, and to come unto the_[ __knowledge__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08673a.htm>) _of the_[ __truth__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>). [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , then, in His great condescension has judged it good to grant to the [ _prayers_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12345b.htm>) of the [ _humble_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07543b.htm>) the [ _salvation_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>) of the exalted; and assuredly we have many examples of this. Our Lord, too, makes use of the same mode of speech in the [ _Gospel_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06655b.htm>) , when He says to the [ _Pharisees_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11789b.htm>) :  _You_[ __tithe__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14741b.htm>) _mint, and rue, and every herb_. For the [ _Pharisees_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11789b.htm>) did not [ _tithe_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14741b.htm>) what belonged to others, nor all the herbs of all the inhabitants of other lands. **_As, then, in this place we must understand by_**** __every herb,__****_every kind of herbs, so in the former passage we may understand by_**** __all__**[** __men__**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)** __,__****_every sort of men_**. And we may interpret it in any other way we please, **_so long as we are not compelled to_**[** _believe_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02408b.htm>)** _that the_**[** _omnipotent_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11251c.htm>)** _God has willed anything to be done which was not done: for setting aside all ambiguities, if_**** __He has done all that He pleased in heaven and in earth,__****_as the psalmist sings of Him, He certainly did not will to do anything that He has not done_**.

__

_Chapter 104. God, Foreknowing the Sin of the First Man, Ordered His Own Purposes Accordingly._

Wherefore, God would have been willing to preserve even the first man in that state of [ _salvation_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13407a.htm>) in which he was created, and after he had begotten sons to remove him at a fit time, without the intervention of death, to a better place, where he should have been not only free from [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , **but free even from the desire of sinning** , if He had foreseen that man would have the steadfast will to persist in the state of innocence in which he was created. **But as He foresaw that man would make a bad use of his**[** _free-will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>)**, that is, would**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)**, God arranged His own designs rather with a view to do good to man even in his sinfulness, that thus the good will of the Omnipotent might not be made void by the**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)**will of**[** _man_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)**, but might be fulfilled in spite of it**.

__

_Chapter 105. Man Was So Created as to Be Able to Choose Either Good or Evil_** _: in the Future Life, the Choice of Evil Will Be Impossible_** _._

Now it was expedient that man should be **at first so created, as to have it in his power both to will what was right and to will what was wrong** ; not without reward if he willed the former, and not without punishment if he willed the latter. **_But in the future life it shall not be in his power to will_**[** _evil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>)** _; and yet this will constitute no restriction on the freedom of his will. On the contrary, his will shall be much freer when it shall be wholly impossible for him to be the slave of_**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>). We should never think of blaming the will, or saying that it was no will, or that it was not to be called free, when we so desire [ _happiness_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>) , that not only do we shrink from misery, but find it utterly impossible to do otherwise. As, then, the [ _soul_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14153a.htm>) even now finds it impossible to desire unhappiness, **so in future it shall be wholly impossible for it to desire**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>). But God's arrangement was not to be broken, according to which He willed to show how good is a rational being who is able even to refrain from [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , and yet how much better is one who cannot [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) at all; just as that was an inferior sort of [ _immortality_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07687a.htm>) , and yet it was [ _immortality_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07687a.htm>) , when it was possible for man to avoid death, **although there is reserved for the future a more perfect**[** _immortality_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07687a.htm>)**, when it shall be impossible for man to die**.

 _Chapter 106. The Grace of God Was Necessary to Man's Salvation Before the Fall as Well as After It._

The former [ _immortality_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07687a.htm>) man lost through the exercise of his [ _free-will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>) ; the latter he shall obtain through [ _grace_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>) , **whereas, if he had not**[** _sinned_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)**, he should have obtained it by desert**. Even in that case, however, there could have been no merit without [ _grace_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>) ; because, although the mere exercise of [ _man's_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) [ _free-will_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>) was sufficient to bring in [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , **his**[** _free-will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>)**would not have sufficed for his maintenance in righteousness, unless God had assisted it by imparting a portion of His unchangeable goodness**. Just as it is in man's power to die whenever he will (for, not to speak of other means, any one can put an end to himself by simple abstinence from food), **but the mere will cannot preserve life in the absence of food and the other means of life** ; so man in paradise was able of his mere will, simply by abandoning righteousness, to destroy himself; **but to have maintained a life of righteousness would have been too much for his will, unless it had been sustained by the Creator's power. After the fall, however, a more abundant exercise of God's mercy was required, because the will itself had to be freed from the bondage in which it was held by**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)**and death**. And the will owes its freedom in no degree to itself, **but solely to the**[** _grace_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>)**of God which comes by**[** _faith_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05752c.htm>)**in**[** _Jesus Christ_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08374c.htm>) ; so that the very will, through which we accept all the other gifts of God which lead us on to His [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) gift, is itself prepared of the Lord, as the [ _Scripture_](<https://www.newadvent.org/bible/index.html>) says.

 _Chapter 107. Eternal Life, Though the Reward of Good Works, is Itself the Gift of God._

Wherefore, even [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) life itself, **_which is surely the reward of good works_** , the apostle calls the gift of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>).  _For the wages of_[ __sin__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , he says,  _is death; but the gift of God is_[ __eternal__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) _life through_[ __Jesus Christ__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08374c.htm>) _our Lord_. **Wages (****_stipendium_****) is paid as a recompense for military service; it is not a gift** : wherefore he says,  _the wages of_[ __sin__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) _is death,_ **to show that death was not inflicted undeservedly, but as the due recompense of**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>). But a gift, unless it is wholly unearned, is not a gift at all. We are to understand, then, that man's good deserts are themselves the gift of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , **so that when these obtain the recompense of**[** _eternal_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>)**life, it is simply**[** _grace_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>)**given for**[** _grace_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>). Man, therefore, was thus made upright that, though unable to remain in his uprightness without divine help, **he could of his own mere will depart from it**. And whichever of these courses he had chosen, [** _God's_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)****[**_will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**would have been done, either by him, or concerning him**. Therefore, as he chose to do his own will rather than God's, **the**[** _will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15624a.htm>)**of**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)**is fulfilled concerning him; for**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>)**, out of one and the same heap of perdition which constitutes the race of**[** _man_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>)**, makes one vessel to**[** _honor_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07462a.htm>)**, another to dishonor; to**[** _honor_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07462a.htm>)**in mercy, to dishonor in judgment** ; that no one may [ _glory_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06585a.htm>) in man, and consequently not in himself.

__

_Chapter 108. A Mediator Was Necessary to Reconcile Us to God; And Unless This Mediator Had Been God, He Could Not Have Been Our Redeemer._

For we could not be redeemed, even through the one Mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, **if He were not also God**. Now when [ _Adam_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01129a.htm>) was created, he, being a righteous man, had no need of a mediator. But when [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) had placed a wide gulf between God and the [ _human race_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , it was expedient that a Mediator, who alone of the [ _human race_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) was born, lived, and died without [ _sin_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , should reconcile us to [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , and procure even for our bodies a resurrection to [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) life, in order that the [ _pride_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12405a.htm>) of man might be exposed and cured **through the humility of**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) ; that man might be shown how far he had departed from [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , **when God became incarnate to bring him back** ; that an example might be set to disobedient man **in the life of**[** _obedience_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11181c.htm>)**of the God-Man** ; that the fountain of [ _grace_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm>) might be opened **by the Only-begotten taking upon Himself the form of a servant, a form which had no antecedent merit** ; that an earnest of that resurrection of the body which is promised to the redeemed might be given in the resurrection of the Redeemer; that the [ _devil_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm>) might be subdued by the same nature which it was his boast to have deceived, **and yet man not**[** _glorified_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06585a.htm>)**, lest**[** _pride_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12405a.htm>)**should again spring up** ; and, in fine, with a view to all the advantages which the thoughtful can perceive and describe, or perceive without being able to describe, as flowing from the transcendent [ _mystery_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10662a.htm>) of the person of the Mediator.
