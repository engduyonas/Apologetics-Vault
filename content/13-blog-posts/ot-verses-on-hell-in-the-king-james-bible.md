# OT Verses on Hell in the King James Bible

  * [samshmn](<https://www.samshmnthelogy.net/profile/samshmn78303/profile>)
  * Nov 18, 2025
  * 22 min read


The Authorized King James Version (AV) of the Holy Bible renders two different words as hell, namely the Hebrew term _Sheol_ (Greek – _Hades_) and the Greek phrase _Gehenna_.

  


Sheol/Hades typically refers to the abode of the disembodied dead, the place where the spirits/souls of the departed go to when they physically die. In certain instances, it simply means the common grave. And up until Jesus’ physical resurrection and ascension into heaven everyone went to this spiritual place/dimension, with the righteous being in a state of peace and bliss in contrast to the wicked who undergo agony and torment as they remain there until the last day (cf. Genesis 15:15; 25:8-9; 1 Samuel 28:11-19; Luke 16:19-23).

  


Now, however, only the spirits/souls of the wicked enter there, whereas the spirits/souls of the righteous go to be with the risen Lord in heaven (cf. 2 Corinthian 5:1-10; Philippians 1:21, 23; Hebrews 12:22-24; Revelation 6:9-11). Gehenna, on the other hand, is the place where both the body and spirit/soul of a person is sent to experience unending punishment after the general resurrection and judgment day (cf. Matthew 10:28; Mark 9:43-49; Revelation 14:9-11; 19:20; 20:10-15).

  


With that said, we invite the readers to compare the AV’s rendering of the following OT passages with other Bible translations and determine whether these texts either allude or directly refer to these spiritual abodes that the AV calls Hell.

“Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he _is_ just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass. And I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim, and the horse from Jerusalem, and the battle bow shall be cut off: and he shall speak peace unto the heathen: and his dominion _shall be_ from sea _even_ to sea, and from the river _even_ to the ends of the earth. **_As for thee also, by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein_**** __is__**** _no water_**.” Zechariah 9:9-11

  


This last passage is rather interesting since not only did the Lord Jesus fulfill this specific prophesy concerning Zion’s King entering Jerusalem on the foal of a donkey, but he is also the One who saves people from going down into the fiery pit by the blood that he shed to ratify and institute the everlasting covenant of redemption (cf. Matthew 21:1-11; 26:26-28; Luke 22:19-20; John 12:12-16; Hebrews 8:6; 9:11-28; 13:20-21).
