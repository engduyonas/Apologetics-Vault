# Augustine, Origen, & Hell

  * [samshmn](<https://www.samshmnthelogy.net/profile/samshmn78303/profile>)
  * Dec 22, 2025
  * 8 min read


The great St. Augustine was explicit in his articulation that the wicked will suffer never-ending, everlasting conscious torment. This most influential theologian vehemently opposed the doctrine of conditionalism/annihilationism (that the wicked will eventually be annihilated after undergoing a period of suffering).

St. Augustine is also aware that there were many Christians in his day and previously who rejected everlasting conscious torment. These individuals either held to conditionalism or to universalism (that all mankind will eventually be saved). The blessed Father of the Church refutes these claims in his writings, some of which I quote here for the benefit of the readers so they can see his position regarding these issues laid out clearly. All emphasis will be mine.

 _Chapter 109. The State of the Soul During the Interval Between Death and the Resurrection._

During the time, moreover, which intervenes between a man's death and the final resurrection, the [ _soul_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14153a.htm>) dwells in a hidden retreat, where it enjoys rest or suffers affliction just in proportion to the merit it has earned by the life which it led on earth.

 _Chapter 110._**_The Benefit to the Souls of the Dead from the Sacraments and Alms of Their Living Friends_** _._

**Nor can it be denied that the**[** _souls_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14153a.htm>)**of the dead are benefited by the**[** _piety_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12748a.htm>)**of their living friends, who offer the**[** _sacrifice_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13309a.htm>)**of the Mediator, or give**[** _alms_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01328f.htm>)**in the church on their behalf**. But these services are of advantage only to those who during their lives have earned such merit, that services of this kind can help them. For there is a manner of life which is neither so good as not to require these services after death, nor so bad that such services are of no avail after death; **there is, on the other hand, a kind of life so good as not to require them; and again, one so bad that when life is over they render no help. Therefore, it is in this life that all the merit or demerit is acquired, which can either relieve or aggravate a man's sufferings after this life. No one, then, need hope that after he is dead he shall obtain merit with God which he has neglected to secure here**. And accordingly it is plain that the services which the church celebrates for the dead are in no way opposed to the apostle's words:  _For we must all appear before the judgment-seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he has done, whether it be good or bad;_ for the merit which renders such services as I speak of profitable to a [ _man_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , is earned while he lives in the body. It is not to every one that these services are profitable. And why are they not profitable to all, except because of the different kinds of lives that men lead in the body? **When, then, sacrifices either of the altar or of**[** _alms_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01328f.htm>)**are offered on behalf of all the**[** _baptized_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02258b.htm>)**dead, they are thank-offerings for the very good, they are propitiatory offerings for the not very bad** , and in the case of the very bad, even though they do not assist the dead, they are a species of consolation to the living. And where they are profitable, **_their benefit consists either in obtaining a full remission of_**[** _sins_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)** _, or at least in making the condemnation more tolerable_**.

__

_Chapter 111. After the Resurrection There Shall Be Two Distinct Kingdoms, One of Eternal Happiness, the Other of Eternal Misery._

After the resurrection, however, when the final, universal judgment has been completed, there shall be two kingdoms, each with its own distinct boundaries, the one Christ's, the other the [ _devil's_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm>) ; the one consisting of the [ _good_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06636b.htm>) , the other of the bad — both, however, consisting of [ _angels_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01476d.htm>) and men. **The former shall have no will, the latter no power, to**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)**, and neither shall have any power to choose death** ; but the former shall live [ _truly_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) and happily in [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) life, **the latter shall drag a miserable**[** _existence_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05543b.htm>)**in**[** _eternal_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>)**death without the power of dying; for the life and the death shall both be without end**. But among the former there shall be degrees of [ _happiness_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>) , one being more pre-eminently [ _happy_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>) than another; **and among the latter there shall be degrees of misery, one being more endurably miserable than another**.

 _Chapter 112._******_There is No Ground in Scripture for the Opinion of Those Who Deny the Eternity of Future Punishments_**.

**It is in vain, then, that some,****_INDEED VERY MANY_****, make moan over the**[** _eternal_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>)**punishment, and perpetual, unintermitted torments of the lost, and say they do not**[** _believe_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02408b.htm>)**it shall be so;****__not, indeed, that they directly oppose themselves to__**[** __Holy Scripture__**](<https://www.newadvent.org/bible/index.html>)**, but, at the suggestion of their own feelings, they soften down everything that seems hard, and give a milder turn to statements which they think are rather designed to terrify than to be received as literally**[** _true_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>). _For Hath God_ they say, _forgotten to be gracious?_  _Has He in_[ __anger__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) _shut up His tender mercies?_ Now, they read this in one of the [ _holy_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07386a.htm>) psalms. **But without**[** _doubt_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05141a.htm>)**we are to understand it as spoken of those who are elsewhere called**** _vessels of mercy_** , because even they are freed from misery not on account of any merit of their own, **but solely through the pity of**[** _God_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>). Or, if the men we speak of insist that this passage applies to all [ _mankind_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , there is no reason why they should therefore suppose **that there will be an end to the punishment of those of whom it is said,****__These shall go away into everlasting punishment__****; for this shall end in the same manner and at the same time as the**[** _happiness_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>)**of those of whom it is said,****__but the righteous unto life__**[** __eternal__**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>). But let them suppose, if the thought gives them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are, at certain intervals, in some degree assuaged. For even in this case the [ _wrath_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , that is, their condemnation (for it is this, and not any disturbed feeling in the [ _mind_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10321a.htm>) of God that is called His [ _wrath_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>)), abides upon them; that is, His [ _wrath_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) , though it still remains, does not shut up His tender mercies; though His tender mercies are exhibited, not in putting an end to their [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) punishment, but in mitigating, or in granting them a respite from, their torments; for the psalm does not say,  _to put an end to His_[ __anger__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) _,_ or,  _when His_[ __anger__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) _is passed by,_ but  _in His_[ __anger__](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) _._ Now, if this [ _anger_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm>) stood alone, or if it [ _existed_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05543b.htm>) in the smallest conceivable degree, yet to be lost out of the [ _kingdom of God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08646a.htm>) , to be an exile from the city of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , to be alienated from the life of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , to have no share in that great goodness which [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) has laid up for them that [ _fear_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06021a.htm>) Him, and has wrought out for them that trust in Him, would be a punishment so great, that, supposing it to be [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) , no torments that we [ _know_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08673a.htm>) of, continued through as many ages as man's imagination can conceive, could be compared with it.

 _Chapter 113._**_The Death of the Wicked Shall Be Eternal in the Same Sense as the Life of the Saints_**.

**This perpetual death of the**[** _wicked_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm>) , then, that is, their alienation from the life of [ _God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm>) , **shall abide for ever** , and shall be common to them all, whatever men, prompted by their [ _human_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) affections, may conjecture as to a variety of punishments, or as to a mitigation or intermission of their woes; **just as the**[** _eternal_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>)**life of the**[** _saints_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04171a.htm>)**shall abide for ever** , and shall be common to them all, whatever grades of rank and [ _honor_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07462a.htm>) there may be among those who shine with an harmonious effulgence. ([_The Enchiridion_](<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1302.htm>))

It is interesting that St. Augustine acknowledged that there were many that opposed ECT not of spite for Scripture, since they loved and honored the Holy Scriptures.

Elsewhere, St. Augustine acknowledges that Origen was an earlier theologian/apologist who even believed that Satan himself and his angels would eventually be saved, a view which he claimed was condemned by the Church:

Chapter 17.— Of Those Who Fancy that No Men Shall Be Punished Eternally.

I must now, I see, enter the lists of amicable controversy **with those tender-hearted**[** _Christians_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03712a.htm>)**who decline to**[** _believe_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02408b.htm>)**that any** , or that all of those whom the infallibly just Judge may pronounce worthy of the punishment of [ _hell_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07207a.htm>) , shall suffer [ _eternally_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) , **_and who suppose that they shall be delivered after a fixed term of punishment, longer or shorter according to the amount of each man's_**[** _sin_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>). **In respect of this matter,**[**_Origen_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11306b.htm>)**was even more indulgent;****_for he_**[** _believed_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02408b.htm>)** _that even the_**[** _devil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm>)** _himself and his_**[** _angels_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01476d.htm>)**, after suffering those more severe and prolonged pains which their**[** _sins_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>)**deserved,****_should be delivered from their torments_****, and associated with the**[** _holy_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07386a.htm>)****[**_angels_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01476d.htm>)**.****_But the_**[** _Church_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03744a.htm>)** _, not without reason, condemned him for this and other_**[** _errors_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05525a.htm>)** _, especially for his theory of the ceaseless alternation of_**[** _happiness_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>)** _and misery, and the interminable transitions from the one state to the other at fixed periods of ages_** ; for in this theory he lost even the credit of being merciful, by allotting to the [ _saints_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04171a.htm>) real miseries for the expiation of their [ _sins_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm>) , and false [ _happiness_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>) , which brought them no [ _true_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) and secure [ _joy_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07131b.htm>) , that is, no fearless assurance of [ _eternal_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>) blessedness. **Very different, however, is the**[** _error_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05525a.htm>)**we speak of, which is dictated by the tenderness of these**[** _Christians_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03712a.htm>)**who suppose that the sufferings of those who are condemned in the judgment will be temporary, while the blessedness of all who are sooner or later set**[** _free will_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06259a.htm>)**be**[** _eternal_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05551b.htm>). Which opinion, if it is [ _good_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06636b.htm>) and [ _true_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) because it is merciful, will be so much the better and [ _truer_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) in proportion as it becomes more merciful. Let, then, this fountain of mercy be extended, and flow forth even to the lost [ _angels_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01476d.htm>) , and let them also be set free, at least after as many and long ages as seem fit! Why does this stream of mercy flow to all the [ _human race_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm>) , and dry up as soon as it reaches the angelic? **And yet they dare not extend their pity further, and propose the deliverance of the**[** _devil_**](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm>)**himself**. Or if any one is bold enough to do so, he does indeed put to shame their charity, but is himself convicted of [ _error_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05525a.htm>) that is more unsightly, and a wresting of God's [ _truth_](<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm>) that is more perverse, in proportion as his clemency of sentiment seems to be greater. ([_City of God_](<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1201.htm>) , [_Book 21_](<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120121.htm>) Of the end reserved for the city of the devil, namely, the eternal punishment of the damned; and of the arguments which unbelief brings against it.)

It is interesting that, though he calls conditionalism/annihilationism an error, St. Augustine doesn’t say that it is a damnable heresy condemned by the Church, as he did with Origen’s universalist position. It seems that the former view was tolerated, whereas the latter was outright rejected as heresy.
